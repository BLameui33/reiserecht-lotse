eSIM & Roaming: How to stay online worldwide without a costly surprise

Anyone travelling outside Europe knows the problem: the inclusive data allowance from your home tariff no longer applies and mobile providers’ roaming charges can rise dramatically. Often, just a few megabytes can cost several pounds. The solution to this problem is the eSIM (embedded SIM).

With an eSIM, you simply download a digital mobile profile for your destination country – without the hassle of swapping cards at the airport. In our guide, you will learn how to keep track of your data allowance, check whether your smartphone is compatible and find the best tariff for your next adventure.

The ultimate eSIM & roaming checklist

So that you do not suddenly find yourself without internet access or facing unexpected charges when you arrive at your holiday destination, work through these points before departure:

Checklist

  • Check compatibility: Does my smartphone have an eSIM chip? (Use our checker above.)
  • Check for a device lock: Is the phone unlocked for other networks (no SIM lock)?
  • Disable home data roaming: Before departure, switch off data roaming for your main SIM in the settings.
  • Buy & install the eSIM on Wi-Fi: Ideally, set up the eSIM at home or on hotel Wi-Fi using a QR code.
  • Assign the data line: In your smartphone settings, set the new eSIM as the primary source for "Mobile Data".

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about using an eSIM abroad

What is an eSIM and how does it differ from a normal SIM?
An eSIM (embedded SIM) is a chip built directly into your smartphone that can be digitally loaded with mobile network profiles. You no longer need a physical plastic card and can activate tariffs, especially for travel abroad, in seconds via QR code or app.
Can I use my normal SIM card and an eSIM at the same time?
Yes, most modern smartphones support dual SIM. You can keep your physical SIM card active for calls and texts on your usual number (be careful of roaming charges when receiving calls) and use the eSIM specifically for mobile data in your destination country.
Does my smartphone support eSIM?
Most newer smartphones, such as the iPhone from the XS series onwards, Samsung Galaxy S20 and later, or Google Pixel from generation 3, support eSIM. To be 100% sure, use our free eSIM checker at the top of this page.
How do I avoid roaming charges outside Europe?
The most important step: before entering a non-European country, switch off data roaming for your main SIM card. Buy and install a local or regional eSIM in advance. As soon as you arrive, enable data roaming only for the newly created eSIM profile in your settings.
