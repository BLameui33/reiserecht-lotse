Your rights with Croatia Airlines under UK Regulation 261 (UK261)

If you fly with Croatia Airlines and are affected by a significant delay, a cancellation or denied boarding, you may benefit from one of the strongest passenger protection regimes available: UK Regulation 261 (UK261). This regulation requires airlines to make fixed compensation payments that are entirely independent of the ticket price. Even if you booked a budget flight for £30, you may still be entitled to hundreds of pounds if your flight was disrupted.

How much compensation can you claim from Croatia Airlines?

The amount of statutory compensation does not depend on the ticket price and generally not on the length of the delay alone, but primarily on the flight distance:

  • Short-haul (up to 1,500 km): £220 compensation (e.g. London to Mallorca).
  • Medium-haul (1,500 to 3,500 km): £350 compensation (e.g. London to Tenerife).
  • Long-haul (over 3,500 km): £520 compensation (e.g. London to New York).

Important: In the case of a delay, entitlement to this payment generally arises only if you arrive at your final destination at least 3 hours late. If departure is delayed by three hours but the pilot makes up time in the air and lands only 2 hours and 50 minutes late, the claim usually falls away.

The big excuse: "extraordinary circumstances"

When you send your claim letter to Croatia Airlines, the airline will often try to rely on so-called "extraordinary circumstances". If these apply, the airline may not have to pay compensation. However, as a passenger you should not simply accept this at face value, because the case law in this area is strict.

  • These ARE extraordinary circumstances: severe weather (for example a storm at the airport), strikes by third parties (for example air traffic control or airport security staff), political instability, airspace closures or security threats.
  • These are NOT extraordinary circumstances: a technical defect on the aircraft (even if it arises unexpectedly), crew absence due to illness, lack of fuel or a strike by the airline's own staff (for example pilots employed by Croatia Airlines). In these cases, compensation is generally payable.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about flight compensation

How long do I have to claim compensation?

Time limits for bringing a claim can vary depending on the legal basis and the circumstances. For claims under UK Regulation 261 (UK261), it is best to act promptly and, if necessary, check the applicable limitation period for your route and jurisdiction.

Do I have to accept vouchers from Croatia Airlines?

Absolutely not. Airlines often try to fob off frustrated passengers at the airport with travel vouchers because this helps their cash flow. Under UK Regulation 261 (UK261), however, you have the right to receive compensation as a cash payment or by bank transfer.

What about care and assistance at the airport?

Quite separately from financial compensation, Croatia Airlines must provide care and assistance during long waits at the airport. This means free meals, refreshments and means of communication. If the flight is moved to the next day, the airline must pay for hotel accommodation and transport to and from it. Keep receipts for food or taxis if the airline failed to arrange this.

Important notes on refunds from Croatia Airlines

Croatia Airlines is Croatia's national carrier, headquartered in Zagreb with its main hub at Zagreb Airport. As an EU carrier, many claims for flight delay, cancellation or denied boarding fall under UK Air Passenger Rights (UK261) or EU Regulation 261/2004. Lost or damaged baggage may additionally be covered by the Montreal Convention. Refunds of unused taxes and fees may also be possible. Jurisdiction lies in Zagreb, so claims against the airline are generally assessed under Croatian law alongside applicable UK/EU rules.
