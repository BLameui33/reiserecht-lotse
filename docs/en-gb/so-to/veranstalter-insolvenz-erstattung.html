Tour operator insolvent: Your rights and the path to your money

Package holiday operators must protect their customers against their own insolvency. The basis is the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 (implementing the EU Directive in the UK). In the event of insolvency, payments already made are refunded, and return journeys are secured for ongoing holidays. You receive proof of this protection as an ATOL certificate (or ABTA/TTA bond), usually as part of your booking confirmation. However, not everything is protected, and not every path makes sense for every payment method.

The protection certificate: Your most important proof

The protection certificate proves that your payments are secured and names the protection provider you should contact. You will usually find it in your booking confirmation or invoice, mostly in the section after the payment details. Keep it safe and have it ready when registering your claim. If it is missing, ask the operator, your online account, or the travel agent you booked through.

Registering your claim with the protection provider

Often, the protection provider sets up its own process, with a refund form, evidence, and a deadline. You usually need the protection certificate, booking confirmation, and proof of your payments. If multiple people are travelling, a power of attorney from fellow travellers may be required. In practice, the protection provider does not always reach everyone affected, so check their website and register actively. The letter above serves as a registration or supplement to the form.

Standalone bookings: Insolvency practitioner

Standalone travel services, such as a flight only or just a hotel stay, are usually not protected by the package travel insolvency scheme. You can register your claim in the insolvency table with the insolvency practitioner (administrator/liquidator). Whether and to what extent money is paid out is uncertain and often takes a long time. The tool creates the registration for this. Check whether the insolvency court prescribes a specific form or procedure.

Paid by card: Chargeback and Section 75

If you paid by credit or debit card, you can ask your bank to reclaim the payment because the service is not being provided due to insolvency. For credit cards, this is covered by Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 (for amounts between £100 and £30,000), and for debit cards, it is a chargeback scheme run by the card networks. The bank checks if other protection applies. It is important that you are not compensated twice: inform the bank and protection provider about any refund received. For package holidays, the protection provider is usually the first point of contact. A chargeback or Section 75 claim can be an additional option, especially if the refund is delayed. General information on deadlines and the process can be found in our guide Reclaiming payments.

You are already travelling

For ongoing package holidays, the return journey is part of the protection. Contact the emergency number of the protection provider or administrator and follow their instructions. Only pay additional costs after consulting them, keep all receipts, and claim additional costs later using the letter above.

Holidays not yet taken

In the event of insolvency, upcoming holidays are often cancelled. Clarify whether you want to accept alternative services offered and get the cancellation confirmed in writing. Only accept vouchers or rebookings if the protection is clearly regulated. You can find information on this in the notification from the protection provider.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about tour operator insolvency

Do I get my money back if my tour operator goes bust?

For package holidays and Linked Travel Arrangements, operators must protect their customers against insolvency. Payments already made are then refunded by the protection provider, and return journeys are organised for ongoing holidays. Standalone bookings, such as a flight only or just a hotel stay, are usually not protected.

What is a protection certificate and where do I find it?

The protection certificate (e.g. ATOL certificate) is the proof of insolvency protection for your package holiday. It is usually in the booking confirmation or invoice, mostly in the section after the payment details, and names the protection provider you should contact. Keep it safe.

Do I have to register my claim myself?

In many cases, the protection provider contacts those affected or sets up a claims process, but in practice, they do not always reach everyone. Therefore, check the protection provider's website and register actively if necessary. For standalone bookings, you must register your claim with the insolvency practitioner.

Can I additionally request a chargeback if I paid by card?

This is possible if you paid by credit or debit card and the service is not provided due to insolvency. For credit cards, Section 75 may apply; for debit cards, the chargeback scheme applies. The bank checks if other protection applies, and double compensation is excluded. For package holidays, the protection provider is usually the first point of contact.

What if I only booked a hotel or flight separately?

Standalone travel services are not protected by the package holiday insolvency scheme. You can register your claim with the insolvency practitioner, but whether and to what extent money is paid out is uncertain. If you paid by card, a chargeback or Section 75 claim via your bank is also advisable.

What to do if I am already on holiday when the insolvency happens?

Contact the emergency number of the protection provider or administrator and follow their instructions. The return journey is part of the protection. Only pay additional costs after consulting them and keep all receipts so you can claim additional costs later.

Important notices before sending

Primarily use the forms and portals of the protection provider, if available, and adhere to deadlines. Only send copies of your documents and keep the originals. Secure your message with a timestamp and get confirmation of receipt. Inform other parties if you have already received money to avoid double payments.

Legal bases and information sources

The content of this page is based on the following publicly accessible sources. The instructions from the protection provider and the insolvency announcements in the individual case remain authoritative.

  • Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018: Obliges operators to provide insolvency protection, in particular the refund of payments made and repatriation.
    Text on legislation.gov.uk
  • Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 (UK): Protects credit card purchases between £100 and £30,000 if the supplier goes bust or breaches contract.
    Official text on legislation.gov.uk
  • Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) - ATOL scheme: Protection provider for many flight-inclusive package holidays in the UK.
    Information from the CAA
  • Insolvency Act 1986 (UK): Regulates the registration of claims with the insolvency practitioner.
    Official text on legislation.gov.uk
