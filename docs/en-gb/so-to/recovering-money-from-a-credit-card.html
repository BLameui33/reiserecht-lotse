Getting your money back from banks and card providers: Your options

When paying online for holidays or travel bookings, your options for reclaiming a disputed payment vary greatly depending on the payment method. What matters is how you paid, when the charge occurred, and why you are complaining. The generator above considers exactly these three points.

Reclaiming credit card payments: How the process works

For credit card purchases between £100 and £30,000, you are protected under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974, which makes the card issuer jointly liable with the merchant. For amounts outside this range or for debit cards, you can request a Chargeback via the card scheme (Visa, Mastercard). Important: Chargeback is a scheme rule, not a statutory right, whereas Section 75 is a legal right. Credits can be provisional and reversed if the merchant provides compelling evidence. The process can take weeks or months.

Typical travel cases

Common issues include bookings where the hotel doesn't exist, flights or services not provided, or car hire deposits not released after return. Cancellations where a refund was promised but never paid, and duplicate charges also occur. For package holidays with defects, the Package Travel Regulations 2018 apply; see our guide on hotel defects.

Attempting to resolve with the merchant

Before a bank acts, they usually want to know if you tried to resolve the issue with the merchant. Write to them, state the facts, set a reasonable deadline, and keep all correspondence. If you receive no reply or a refusal, attach it as proof.

Unauthorised payments and card fraud

If you didn't authorise a payment, block your card immediately and report it to your bank. Under the Payment Services Regulations 2017 (PSRs), the bank must refund unauthorised transactions by the end of the next business day. Exceptions apply if you acted with gross negligence or if fraud is suspected against you. Report fraud to Action Fraud (0300 123 2040).

Reclaiming Direct Debits

Under the UK Direct Debit Guarantee, you are entitled to an immediate and full refund from your bank for any Direct Debit you dispute. You don't need to provide a reason. The bank must refund you instantly and then reclaim the money from the merchant.

Reversing bank transfers

A completed Faster Payment is generally final. Your bank can request a recall, but success depends on the receiving bank and beneficiary. If you were tricked into sending money (Authorised Push Payment or APP fraud), new PSR reimbursement rules (from Oct 2024) may entitle you to a refund of up to £85,000.

How to submit your claim

Methods vary by bank. Many offer a dispute form in their app or online banking, while others accept claims via post or phone. Ask for the current submission path and get a reference number.

If the bank refuses

If your bank refuses, ask for a written explanation (a "deadlock letter"). You can then escalate your case to the Financial Ombudsman Service (FOS) within 6 months. Our guide is available at Submission tips & Ombudsman.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) on reclaiming payments

Can I get a credit card payment back if the hotel or service didn't exist?

Yes. For credit card purchases between £100 and £30,000, you are protected under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974, making the card issuer jointly liable. For other amounts or debit cards, you can request a Chargeback via the card scheme.

How long do I have to dispute a card payment?

For Chargebacks, the scheme guide is typically 120 days from the transaction or service date. However, Section 75 claims can be made up to 6 years after the breach of contract in England and Wales. For unauthorised transactions, you must report them without undue delay, with a maximum limit of 13 months under the Payment Services Regulations.

Do I have to contact the merchant first?

Generally, yes. Banks expect a documented attempt to resolve the issue. However, for Section 75 claims, you can claim directly against the card issuer. For unauthorised transactions (fraud), contact your bank immediately.

How do I get a Direct Debit back?

Under the UK Direct Debit Guarantee, you are entitled to an immediate and full refund from your bank for any Direct Debit you dispute, without needing to provide a reason. Simply contact your bank.

Can I reverse a bank transfer?

A completed Faster Payment or CHAPS transfer is generally final. Your bank can request a recall, but the receiving bank and beneficiary must cooperate. If you were a victim of Authorised Push Payment (APP) fraud, new PSR rules may entitle you to reimbursement.

What to do in case of card fraud?

Block your card immediately via your bank's app or helpline. Report the fraud to Action Fraud (0300 123 2040) and notify your bank without delay. Under the Payment Services Regulations 2017, you are generally entitled to a refund for unauthorised transactions by the end of the next business day.

Important notes before sending

Only submit copies and keep a full copy of your claim. Black out full card numbers on receipts. Don't complain prematurely: if a refund was promised, wait a reasonable time but watch your bank's deadlines. A Chargeback does not replace cancelling a subscription.
