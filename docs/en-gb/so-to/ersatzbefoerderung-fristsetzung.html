Flight cancelled: What the airline owes you and how to enforce alternative transport

If your flight is cancelled, under the UK Flight Compensation Regulations (UK261, retained EU Regulation 261/2004) you have the right to choose, regardless of the cause: a refund of the ticket or alternative transport to the final destination under comparable conditions, at the earliest opportunity or at a later date of your choice (Article 8). This is in addition to the right to care (Article 9). In practice, airlines often remain inactive, especially at the airport or when hotlines are overloaded. This is exactly what the deadline notice is for.

Alternative transport by train, taxi or hire car

The airline may, in principle, organise the onward travel using another mode of transport, as long as it is reasonable and gets you to your destination in a reasonable time. This is often the fastest solution for shorter distances or cancellations at a connecting airport. It is important that the airline makes the choice or agrees to it. Therefore, demand a specific alternative transport with departure or take-off time and state which mode of transport is suitable for you.

Setting a deadline: Why it is important

Anyone who books themselves without giving the airline an opportunity to remedy the situation risks not having the costs reimbursed. The written deadline shows that you gave the airline the chance to fulfil its obligation. There is no statutory minimum deadline. What matters is what is reasonable under the circumstances, for example how urgently you need to arrive and how quickly alternatives are booked out. At the airport, one to three hours is common. Use the live calculator above to insert the time directly into the text.

Booking yourself: Risks and caution regarding costs

Reimbursement for self-organised onward travel is not guaranteed in every case. Airlines often refuse if the costs are unreasonably high (e.g., business class instead of economy, taxi over very long distances instead of train) or if the deadline notice is missing. Therefore, choose the cheapest reasonable option that gets you to your destination in a reasonable time, and keep tickets and payment receipts.

What you should document on site

Photos of the departure board with the time, screenshots of the airline app or the cancellation email, written confirmation of the disruption, names and statements of staff, your deadline notice with the time it was sent, and the prices of alternatives at the time of booking. Also request written confirmation of the cancellation stating the reason.

Compensation and limitation periods

The alternative costs must be distinguished from the fixed compensation (Article 7). This amounts to £220, £350 or £520 depending on the distance and does not apply, among other things, in extraordinary circumstances. Check our guide on flight delays and cancellations to see if you are entitled to it. In the UK, claims under the regulations are generally subject to a limitation period of six years in England and Wales (or five years in Scotland). Nevertheless, submit your claim promptly.

Package holiday instead of individual flight

If the flight is part of a package holiday, also contact your tour operator or the local holiday representative. You are then additionally entitled to rights under the Package Travel Regulations. You can find information in our guide to travel defects.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about alternative transport

Can I book a train or taxi myself for a cancelled flight?

Whether the airline will reimburse the costs is not guaranteed in every case. It is best to set a reasonable written deadline for the airline to provide alternative transport, and only book yourself afterwards if nothing reasonable is offered. Document everything and keep costs within reason.

What is a reasonable deadline?

There is no fixed statutory deadline. Reasonable depends on the situation, such as urgency and availability of alternatives. At the airport, deadlines of one to three hours are common. Do not rely on verbal agreements; record the deadline in writing.

Do I also have a right to alternative transport in case of a delay?

In the event of a mere delay, the UK flight compensation regulations primarily provide for care and, from five hours of delay, a ticket refund. A right to alternative transport under the regulations exists mainly in cases of cancellation and denied boarding. Nevertheless, ask the airline about rebooking.

Will the airline also provide meals and a hotel?

In case of cancellation, denied boarding, and major delays, the airline must offer care: meals and refreshments, two means of communication, and if necessary, a hotel stay including transfers. This generally applies even in extraordinary circumstances such as severe weather or strikes.

Is the compensation the same as the reimbursement of alternative costs?

No. The fixed compensation under Article 7 of the regulations (£220 to £520) is a separate claim and depends, among other things, on the distance and whether extraordinary circumstances applied. Reimbursement of your alternative transport costs may be considered in addition.

What should I do if I booked a package holiday?

Also contact your tour operator or the holiday representative. For package holidays, the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 apply additionally, and the tour operator is your contracting party for resolving the issue.

Important notes before sending

Send the deadline notice via a method that leaves a timestamp (email, contact form with confirmation, chat with history). Only book alternatives if you really need to continue your journey, and prefer flexible fares. Keep the original documents and only send copies to the airline later.
