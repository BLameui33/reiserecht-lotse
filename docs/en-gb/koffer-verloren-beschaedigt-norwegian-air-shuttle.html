Warning: strict legal deadlines apply. Without this official report, Norwegian will reject almost every claim.

Baggage missing or damaged: your rights under the Montreal Convention

The so-called Montreal Convention is an international treaty that regulates airline liability for problems with checked baggage in a largely uniform way worldwide. Whether your suitcase was damaged during handling on a flight with Norwegian, arrived late at your destination or disappeared completely, the airline is liable regardless of fault up to a statutory limit of 1,288 Special Drawing Rights (SDRs). This artificial currency basket corresponds to roughly €1,500 to €1,600 per passenger.

Delayed baggage: what counts as an "emergency purchase"?

If you arrive at your holiday destination and your suitcase is not there, you may buy essential items at Norwegian’s expense (so-called replacement purchases). The key legal principle here is the duty to mitigate loss. You cannot simply go to the nearest luxury boutique. Reimbursable items are those genuinely necessary for the purpose of your trip. These include toiletries (toothbrush, shower gel), underwear and reasonable clothing (for example, a replacement suit for an urgent business meeting or swimwear and a shirt for a beach holiday). Keep all receipts carefully and reclaim the costs using our template letter.

Total loss: when the suitcase is gone for good

If your baggage does not reappear despite extensive searching, it is legally deemed lost after exactly 21 days (total loss). From day 21 onwards, you are entitled to claim the full replacement value or current value of the suitcase as well as the value of its entire contents (up to the maximum limit of around €1,600) from Norwegian. It is best to prepare a detailed packing list from memory and, where available, attach old purchase receipts.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about baggage loss with Norwegian

What happens if my suitcase is delayed on the return flight?

If you arrive home (at your place of residence) and your suitcase is missing, you will generally not be entitled to make emergency purchases. Courts and airlines usually assume that you have enough toothbrushes and clothes at home. Permanent damage or a total loss of the suitcase can of course still be reimbursed.

How do I prove the value of my suitcase?

Ideally, you still have the purchase receipt for the suitcase or the valuables inside it. However, Norwegian will usually apply a deduction for wear and tear when reimbursing you. If the suitcase is physically destroyed, it is best to obtain written confirmation from a local luggage repair specialist that a repair would not be economically worthwhile.

Do I have to send the original receipts by post?

No, never send original purchase receipts to the airline by post. Always include only clear copies or scans with your letter. The airline is legally required to accept copies. Keep the originals safe at home.

Important notes on reimbursements with Norwegian

Norwegian is a Norwegian low-cost airline focused on European and transatlantic routes, based at Oslo-Gardermoen Airport. Although the airline is headquartered in Norway, UK Air Passenger Rights (UK261) or EU Regulation 261/2004 apply to many of its flights, giving rise to claims for delay or cancellation. For baggage problems, such as lost or delayed suitcases, the Montreal Convention applies. Norwegian's registered office and legal jurisdiction is Fornebu, near Oslo. Taxes and fees may also be reclaimable on cancelled bookings, in certain circumstances.
