Successfully enforce a price reduction with Marella Cruises (TUI)

A cruise is usually a package holiday. This means your rights are generally governed by the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 and related consumer protections. If Marella Cruises (TUI) does not provide the promised service, you may be entitled to a holiday price reduction. Industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation can serve as an important point of reference.

Typical issues under ABTA compensation guidelines

  • Route change: If a port call is cancelled, you may often be entitled to a reduction of around 20% to 30% of the relevant daily holiday price.
  • Cabin: Persistent diesel fumes or noise from the onboard disco directly above the cabin may justify reductions of up to 25%.
  • Pool/leisure facilities: If the main pool is closed for several days, a reduction of around 10% is often appropriate.

FAQ: Cruise complaint

How long do I have to report problems after the holiday?

You will generally have time to bring a claim, but limitation periods and contractual notice requirements can vary. As a practical matter, we recommend raising your complaint as soon as possible and ideally within 14 days of returning, as this makes evidence easier to preserve.

Do I have to offset onboard credit against my claim?

If you have already spent the credit, it will often be deducted from the final claim amount. If you refused it, you should claim the full amount in cash.

Important notes on refunds with Marella Cruises (TUI)

Marella Cruises (owned by TUI) offers modern premium cruises with a strong focus on the Mediterranean, Caribbean, and Asia. Under the Package Travel Regulations, if you encounter travel defects such as itinerary changes, missed ports, or significant shortcomings on board, you should report these to the guest relations desk immediately to demand a remedy. Depending on the severity of the issue, claims for a price reduction or compensation for loss of enjoyment may be applicable.

Legal basis

  • Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018: Rights of travellers where package holiday services are not provided as contracted.
  • UK Consumer Rights Act 2015: General consumer protection framework relevant to service quality and remedies.
  • ABTA guidance: Industry-standard reference points for assessing compensation levels in travel complaints.
