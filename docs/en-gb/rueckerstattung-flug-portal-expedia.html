The OTA trap: why Expedia Flights delays your refund

Anyone who books a flight through portals such as Expedia Flights often saves £10 or £20 at the time of booking. But when a cancellation happens, that small discount becomes an expensive trap. Because you have a contract with the intermediary rather than paying the airline directly, the airline’s refund is returned to the intermediary’s account when the booking is cancelled.

The "ping-pong game" between the airline and Expedia Flights

Has the airline cancelled the flight and already initiated the refund? Then your money is already with Expedia Flights. If you ask there, you will usually receive only standardised replies: "We are waiting for confirmation from the airline." This is often a deliberate delaying tactic to use the liquidity of thousands of customers. Legally, you are entitled to payment once the intermediary has received the money from the airline.

Your rights under UK261 and the Consumer Rights Act 2015

Although UK Regulation 261 (UK261) requires ticket costs to be refunded within 7 days, portals such as Expedia Flights often try to retain additional "processing fees".

  • Full refund: If the airline cancels the flight, you are entitled to 100% of the ticket price paid.
  • Place them in default: By setting a written deadline (our PDF), you place Expedia Flights in default. Only then can you claim statutory interest for late payment or instruct a solicitor, whose costs the portal may have to bear.
  • No vouchers: You do not have to accept travel vouchers from Expedia Flights. You are entitled to cash.

Frequently asked questions about refunds from booking portals

The airline says the money is already with the portal. Expedia Flights denies it. Who is lying?

In 95% of cases, the airline is telling the truth. Airlines almost always refund to the original method of payment. Because Expedia Flights paid for the flight on your behalf using a corporate credit card, the money ends up there. The portal then has to allocate that incoming payment to your booking manually – and that is exactly where delays or cost-cutting often occur.

What happens if Expedia Flights does not pay after the deadline?

Once the 14-day deadline has expired, the company is in default. You can then start court proceedings using the Money Claim Online service or escalate the matter to an approved alternative dispute resolution body such as AviationADR where applicable. Because the portal is in default, it may also have to cover your legal costs if the dispute proceeds.

May Expedia Flights charge fees for the cancellation?

If the airline cancelled the flight, the portal may not deduct additional service fees for processing the refund from the ticket price. You are entitled to the full amount refunded by the airline.

Important notes on refunds from Expedia Flights

Expedia is an Online Travel Agency (OTA) and not the operating airline. When booking a flight only, the contract of carriage is usually made directly with the airline. For ticket cancellations and the refund of taxes and fees, OTAs and airlines often pass the responsibility back and forth. Legally, the OTA is not allowed to charge excessive administration fees solely for processing a refund. The UK operations are legally overseen from London. Claims should be carefully reviewed and directed to the correct contractual partner.
