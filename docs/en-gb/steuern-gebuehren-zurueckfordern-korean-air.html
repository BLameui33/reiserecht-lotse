Didn’t fly? Get taxes & fees refunded by Korean Air

Every year, air passengers let millions go unclaimed because they do not know their rights in relation to unused flight tickets. The common misconception is: “My ticket was a cheap basic fare and non-refundable, so the money is gone.” That is not necessarily true. A flight price does not consist solely of the airline’s transport service; it often also includes a significant proportion of third-party charges.

Which charges must the airline refund?

If you cancel a flight or simply miss it (no-show), Korean Air may save money. The following charges generally only have to be paid to third parties if you actually pass through security and use the airport:

  • Airport taxes / passenger charges: Fees charged by the airport per passenger for use of the terminals.
  • Air Passenger Duty: A government levy that becomes payable per passenger who actually flies.
  • Aviation security charges: The cost of cabin baggage and passenger screening on the ground.

The legal position: why you may be entitled

The legal basis for your reclaim in the UK will usually depend on the contract terms and general consumer law, in particular the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015. If the airline keeps charges that were not actually incurred because you did not travel, or relies on unfair terms to block a refund, those terms may be challengeable. You may therefore have a legal basis to request repayment of unused taxes and fees. Our PDF template letter is worded to assert that claim clearly and to address common objections raised by Korean Air.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about flight cancellation

Can Korean Air deduct an administration fee?

Not automatically. Airlines often try to hide flat “cancellation fees” or “administration charges” in their terms and conditions, which can drastically reduce the refund value. Under the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015, unfair terms may be unenforceable. Our generator explicitly requests a refund without such deductions.

How can I find out how much the taxes were?

Your booking confirmation or invoice from Korean Air will usually contain a detailed price breakdown (ticket price plus taxes/surcharges). However, you do not necessarily need to enter the exact amount. Our template letter is designed to require the airline to disclose transparently the amount of the unused charges and refund it.

How long do I have to reclaim the money?

You may have more time than you think. In the UK, limitation periods can vary depending on the legal basis of the claim and the part of the UK involved. Contract claims are often subject to a limitation period of up to six years in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and five years in Scotland. If you are unsure, seek independent legal advice promptly.

Important notes on refunds from Korean Air

Korean Air is South Korea's largest airline and a member of the SkyTeam alliance. Its main hub is Seoul-Incheon Airport (ICN); its registered office and legal jurisdiction is Seoul. On departures from the UK or EU, delays and denied boarding may give rise to compensation and assistance claims under UK Air Passenger Rights (UK261) or EU Regulation 261/2004. Lost or damaged baggage is governed by the Montreal Convention. Wrongly charged taxes and fees can often also be reclaimed. Review your claims promptly and keep supporting evidence.

Official legal sources

A claim for a refund of passenger-specific taxes, charges and surcharges for a flight not taken may be supported by UK consumer and contract law:

  • UK Consumer Rights Act 2015: Consumer contracts and standard terms used by airlines must be fair. If an airline seeks to retain taxes and charges that were not actually incurred, or imposes disproportionate administration fees for processing a refund, those terms may be challengeable under UK consumer law.
    Official text on legislation.gov.uk
  • Citizens Advice guidance on unfair terms and consumer rights: UK consumers can use general consumer rights principles to challenge unfair contract terms and unreasonable deductions. This can be relevant where an airline refuses to refund unused taxes and fees or relies on unclear booking conditions.
    Official guidance on Citizens Advice
  • Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) – airline complaints and passenger rights: The CAA provides official information on air passenger rights in the UK and explains complaint routes where issues with airlines cannot be resolved directly.
    Official passenger guidance from the CAA
