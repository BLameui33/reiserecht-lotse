How to report travel problems correctly for a booking via Your Travel Agent

Booking a package holiday through major online travel agents such as Your Travel Agent is convenient and popular. But what happens if nothing on arrival matches what was promised in the brochure or listing? If the hotel room is affected by mould, building noise ruins your sleep or the buffet is inedible, you may be entitled to a reduction in the holiday price. In the past, it was often complicated to identify the correct legal contact. Thanks to modern consumer protection rules for package holidays in the UK, the process is now much more consumer-friendly.

Your practical route: complain via the portal and the organiser

In the past, travel portals often rejected complaints with the standard line: "We are only the intermediary, please contact the organiser." In practice, however, many travellers still start by complaining through the portal because it is the easiest route and creates a clear written record. The key point is that your rights usually lie against the package holiday organiser under the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018, with additional consumer protections under the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015. Use this practical route to save yourself the hassle of searching for the right contact details.

ABTA guidelines: how much money could I be entitled to?

Our calculator is based on industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation. These are not binding law, but they provide useful guidance on typical reduction rates for a wide range of holiday problems. Courts, complaint bodies and travel businesses may use similar benchmarks when assessing what is fair. For example, a missing sea view may often justify a 10% reduction, while significant building noise or broken air conditioning may justify a higher percentage of the holiday price. By using our tool, you are claiming realistic amounts that are less likely to be rejected than arbitrary demands.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about holiday price reductions

Who pays the refund when I claim a price reduction?

Payment of the calculated refund (based on industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation) is ultimately made by the actual package holiday organiser. Even if you submit the legally valid complaint letter via the intermediary portal (Your Travel Agent), the organiser processes the payment. You should therefore always include your IBAN or bank account details directly in the letter.

Did I also need to report the problem while I was there?

Yes, this is extremely important. The legal duty to report the problem on site still applies. You must give the organiser’s local representative or the hotel reception the opportunity to put things right (for example by offering another room or carrying out a repair). If you do not allow a reasonable opportunity to remedy the issue, you will usually weaken your claim for financial compensation afterwards.

How long do I have to make my complaint?

Under the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 and the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015, consumers should complain as soon as possible after returning, while evidence such as photos and witness statements is still available. Formal limitation periods may apply depending on the nature of the claim, so prompt action is strongly recommended.

Important notes on refunds with Your Travel Agent

Your Travel Agent is a booking portal or travel intermediary and does not execute the booked trip itself. In the event of travel defects, responsibility generally lies with the tour operator or supplier; therefore, defects should be reported immediately on-site to demand a remedy. For cancellations and cancellation fees, the agent is often the correct first point of contact, as they process the booking. The provider's headquarters generally determine the legal jurisdiction. The specific contract and booking terms remain the decisive factor.

Legal basis & recognised guidance

The complaints and price reduction claims in this generator are based on UK package travel law and widely recognised industry guidance:

  • Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018: These UK regulations set out the obligations of package holiday organisers in detail. In particular, they form the legal basis for complaints about lack of conformity, remedies, price reductions and compensation where a package holiday does not match what was agreed.
    Official text on legislation.gov.uk
  • UK Consumer Rights Act 2015: This Act provides important general consumer protections and is relevant where travel services have not been provided with reasonable care and skill or do not match the contract.
    Official text on legislation.gov.uk
  • ABTA guidance on holiday complaints and compensation: Although not binding legislation, ABTA guidance is a widely recognised industry benchmark when assessing fair compensation for specific problems such as building noise, dirt or missing facilities.
    ABTA help and complaints guidance
