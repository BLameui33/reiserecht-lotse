Train Delay & Cancellation: How to claim up to 50% reimbursement

A missed connection or a complete train cancellation is more than just an inconvenience. Fortunately, your passenger rights are clearly protected in the UK through the Delay Repay scheme and the National Rail Conditions of Travel. Depending on the train operator, you may be entitled to compensation if you reach your destination with a qualifying delay. In many cases, this can mean 25% or 50% of the ticket price, and sometimes more for longer delays.

What matters is the arrival time at your booked destination, not just the delay to one individual train along the way. Our portal helps you avoid much of the paperwork and assert your right to compensation in a formally correct manner.

Checklist: Immediate help at the station

If you are currently stranded at the station, follow these steps so that you can enforce your passenger rights more easily later:

Checklist for train delays

  • Get the delay confirmed: Ask station staff or onboard staff to confirm the delay on your ticket or booking where possible.
  • Secure evidence: Photograph the departure board on the platform or take screenshots from the operator’s app.
  • Check alternative travel options: In the event of disruption, you may be allowed to use another train or a reasonable alternative route under the National Rail Conditions of Travel.
  • Keep receipts: Make sure you keep original tickets, taxi receipts or hotel invoices.
  • Request assistance: In cases of significant delay, you may be entitled to appropriate assistance or onward travel arrangements depending on the circumstances and the operator’s policy.

Case studies: When the rail operator must pay

Example 1: Missed connection due to signalling failure

A passenger travels on a regional service that is only 10 minutes late, but as a result misses the booked onward connection. They have to take a later train and arrive 65 minutes late at their destination. Reason: signalling failure.
Outcome: Operational and technical disruption will often fall within the scope of the operator’s Delay Repay scheme. The passenger may be entitled to 25% reimbursement of the ticket price, depending on the operator’s compensation thresholds.

Example 2: Severe weather stops rail services

An intercity train is stranded for 3 hours because a severe storm has brought trees down onto the track.
Outcome: In the UK, many train operators still process compensation under Delay Repay even where the disruption was caused by severe weather, provided the delay meets the relevant threshold. The rail operator may also have to provide assistance or information about alternative travel arrangements.

FAQ: Your passenger rights in detail

Can I simply take a taxi if there is a delay?
Sometimes, but not automatically. If your train is cancelled, the last service has stopped running, or no reasonable alternative is provided, you may in some circumstances be able to recover the cost of a taxi or other onward transport. However, you should first try to speak to station staff or the train operator and keep all receipts.
How long do I have to submit my claim?
This depends on the train operator. Many Delay Repay claims must be submitted within 28 days of the delayed journey, although some operators allow longer. Check the operator’s terms and conditions as soon as possible and do not leave it too late.
Is there a minimum compensation threshold?
Some train operators may set a minimum payment threshold or calculate compensation differently for season tickets and discounted fares. The exact rules depend on the operator’s Delay Repay policy, so it is worth checking the small print before submitting your claim.
