⚠️ Warning: expensive conversion into South African Rand (ZAR)!

In South Africa, you do not pay in pounds. Every time you use your normal bank card, your bank may charge a so-called foreign transaction fee (usually 1.75% to 2.00%).

On top of that, ATMs often charge a flat fee of £4.50 to £6.50 per withdrawal.

Many holidaymakers only notice this creeping drain on their money weeks later, when they check their bank statement at home and find dozens of small foreign currency charges.

Why your bank is so expensive in South Africa

Most current accounts are optimised for use within the UK or familiar payment areas. As soon as you travel beyond that, banks often turn it into a lucrative source of income. There are two main cost traps you need to know about in South Africa:

1. The foreign transaction fee

As payments in South Africa are made in South African Rand (ZAR), your bank has to convert every transaction into pounds, whether in a supermarket, hotel or car hire office. For this internal processing step, almost all traditional banks add a percentage surcharge. The industry norm is around 1.75% to 2.00%. So if you spend the equivalent of £1,000 by card on holiday, your bank may automatically keep up to £20 as a processing charge.

2. Flat ATM charges

It becomes even more expensive if you need cash from an ATM. If you use your normal debit card or a standard credit card, your bank will often charge a fixed fee per transaction, regardless of how much you withdraw. These charges are often at least £4.50 to £6.50 per withdrawal. If you keep taking out small amounts while away, the fees quickly add up.

The ultimate tourist trap: DCC (Dynamic Currency Conversion)

There is one trick you absolutely need to know in South Africa: when you pay by card or withdraw cash, the card terminal or ATM will often ask whether you want to be charged in the local currency (South African Rand (ZAR)) or in pounds sterling (GBP).

Always choose the local currency (South African Rand (ZAR))!
If you choose “in pounds”, the ATM operator or merchant in South Africa carries out the currency conversion (DCC). These rates are usually very poor and can include hidden mark-ups of up to 10%. If you are charged in South African Rand (ZAR), you leave the conversion to Visa or Mastercard at their fair, up-to-date wholesale exchange rates.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about paying in South Africa

Can I pay in South Africa with my debit card?

Cards on domestic-only networks may not work reliably outside Europe. Cards with a Maestro logo or newer Visa/Mastercard debit cards usually work technically. However, they do not protect you from your bank’s often very high foreign transaction and cash withdrawal charges.

Are there any charges that even a travel credit card cannot prevent?

Yes. Some ATM operators in South Africa add their own usage fee (surcharge), which is added directly to the withdrawal amount (for example: “This ATM charges a fee of ...”). This fee is charged by the ATM operator, not by your bank. Even travel credit cards often cannot refund this third-party charge. In that case, simply look for an ATM from a different bank.

Do I still need cash in South Africa?

Even though card payments (Apple Pay, Google Pay and contactless) are becoming more common worldwide, you should still carry some local cash (South African Rand (ZAR)) for tips, small street stalls, taxis or remote areas.
