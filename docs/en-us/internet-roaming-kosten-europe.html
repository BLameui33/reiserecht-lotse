Why international roaming is so expensive

When you travel internationally, your US network provider has to "rent" access from the local cellular networks in Europe. These interconnection fees are passed directly to you. To avoid Pay-As-You-Go fees, providers like AT&T or Verizon offer "International Day Passes" for $10 to $12 per day. While this sounds okay for a weekend, a 14-day vacation means you are paying an extra $140 to $168 just to use the data plan you already pay for at home. It is a massive cash cow for US carriers.

Security on Public Wi-Fi

Many travelers think: "I don't need a data SIM, I'll just use the free Wi-Fi at the hotel or Starbucks." This is highly questionable in terms of security. Open networks can easily be manipulated by criminals (Man-in-the-Middle attacks) to steal your passwords or online banking data. With your own 4G/5G data connection via an eSIM, you surf on a closed, secure cellular network. Plus, you are not tied to the vicinity of a café when you need to call an Uber or look for directions in Europe.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about eSIMs in Europe

Can I keep my WhatsApp number?

Yes, absolutely! WhatsApp is firmly linked to your installed app, not to the physical card currently providing the internet. If you use the eSIM for the data network, WhatsApp won't ask you to change your number. You can message, call, and send pictures via WhatsApp just like always – only much cheaper.

Does my current smartphone support eSIMs?

Almost all modern smartphones are eSIM compatible. This includes all Apple iPhones from the XS model onwards (including the 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 lines), the Samsung Galaxy S-class from the S20 onwards, and all Google Pixel devices from the Pixel 3. You can find a detailed compatibility list directly in the Airalo app.

When should I buy and install the eSIM?

Download the Airalo app and install the eSIM ideally 1 to 2 days before your flight while relaxed in your home Wi-Fi. The package is generally only activated (depending on the plan) and the validity period begins once the eSIM connects to a cell tower in Europe for the first time.
