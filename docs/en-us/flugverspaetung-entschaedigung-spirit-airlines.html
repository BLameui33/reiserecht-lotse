Your Passenger Rights with Spirit Airlines, Inc. under US DOT Regulations

If you are flying with Spirit Airlines, Inc. within, to, or from the United States, you are protected by the passenger rights enforced by the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT). While the U.S. doesn't have a flat-rate compensation system for simple delays like Europe does, recent DOT rulings heavily penalize airlines for overbooking, failing to refund canceled flights, and neglecting passengers during controllable delays.

Automatic Refunds & Ticket Reimbursements

According to strict DOT regulations, you are legally entitled to a prompt and full refund to your original form of payment if your flight is canceled or significantly altered (delayed by more than 3 hours domestically, or 6 hours internationally), and you choose not to accept the alternative travel offered. This applies regardless of the ticket class – even non-refundable Basic Economy tickets must be refunded entirely.

  • Flight Cancellations: Immediate right to a full refund.
  • Significant Delays: If delayed over 3h (domestic) or 6h (international) and you cancel your trip.
  • Baggage Delays: If your bag is significantly delayed, the airline must refund any checked baggage fees you paid.

Involuntary Denied Boarding (Overbooking)

If you show up on time but Spirit Airlines, Inc. bumps you from an oversold flight against your will, you hit the jackpot of U.S. air travel compensation. The DOT strictly mandates cash compensation based on the arrival delay of your rebooked flight:

  • 0 to 1 hour arrival delay: No compensation.
  • 1 to 2 hours domestic (1 to 4 hours int'l): 200% of your one-way fare (up to $1,075).
  • Over 2 hours domestic (over 4 hours int'l): 400% of your one-way fare (up to $2,150).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What is the difference between Controllable and Uncontrollable Delays?

A controllable delay is caused by the airline (e.g., maintenance issues, crew shortages, or aircraft cleaning). In these cases, Spirit Airlines, Inc.’s customer service plan usually guarantees meal vouchers and hotel accommodations if you are delayed overnight. An uncontrollable delay is caused by severe weather or Air Traffic Control (ATC). For uncontrollable delays, the airline is not required to cover your hotel or food, but you still have the right to a refund if you decide not to travel.

Do I have to accept vouchers from Spirit Airlines, Inc.?

Absolutely not! Airlines frequently try to issue travel credits or vouchers because it saves them cash. If the DOT regulations entitle you to a refund (e.g., due to cancellation), you have the absolute legal right to insist on a cash refund to your credit card. You do not have to accept vouchers.

What if I paid for meals or hotels during a controllable delay?

If Spirit Airlines, Inc. caused the delay (controllable) but failed to provide the promised meal vouchers or hotel accommodations, you should keep all your receipts. As a budget carrier with unbundled pricing, Spirit is frequently the subject of complaints about ancillary fees, seat assignments, and schedule changes, but it remains fully subject to DOT rules on refunds and denied boarding compensation.
