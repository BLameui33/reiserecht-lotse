Why International Roaming Is So Expensive

When you travel outside the United States, standard domestic cell plans often trigger steep pay-per-use rates. US carriers like AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile have to pay interconnection fees to local network operators in Tunisia. These international roaming rates can lead to severe bill shock. Even carrier daily passes (costing $10 to $12 per day) can quickly add up over a longer trip while offering restrictive daily high-speed data caps or throttled speeds.

Security on Public Wi-Fi

Many travelers think: "I don't need a data SIM, I'll just use the free Wi-Fi at the hotel or Starbucks." This is highly questionable in terms of security. Open networks can easily be manipulated by criminals (Man-in-the-Middle attacks) to steal your passwords or online banking data. With your own 4G/5G data connection via an eSIM, you surf on a closed, secure cellular network. Plus, you are not tied to the vicinity of a café when you need to call an Uber or look for directions in Tunisia.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about eSIMs in Tunisia

Can I keep my WhatsApp number?

Yes, absolutely! WhatsApp is firmly linked to your installed app, not to the physical card currently providing the internet. If you use the eSIM for the data network, WhatsApp won't ask you to change your number. You can message, call, and send pictures via WhatsApp just like always – only much cheaper.

Does my current smartphone support eSIMs?

Almost all modern smartphones are eSIM compatible. This includes all Apple iPhones from the XS model onwards (including the 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 lines), the Samsung Galaxy S-class from the S20 onwards, and all Google Pixel devices from the Pixel 3. You can find a detailed compatibility list directly in the Holafly app.

When should I buy and install the eSIM?

Download the Holafly app and install the eSIM ideally 1 to 2 days before your flight while relaxed in your home Wi-Fi. The package is generally only activated (depending on the plan) and the validity period begins once the eSIM connects to a cell tower in Tunisia for the first time.
