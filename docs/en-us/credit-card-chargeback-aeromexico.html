Why a Chargeback is Your Strongest Weapon Against Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico)

Dealing with Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico) after a flight cancellation or a ruined hotel stay can be incredibly frustrating. Call centers often stonewall customers, deny refunds, or offer useless travel vouchers instead of returning your hard-earned money. But if you paid with a credit card, you are protected by federal law.

The Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) gives consumers the right to dispute charges for goods or services that were not delivered as agreed. When you file a chargeback, your bank investigates the claim. If they side with you, they physically pull the funds back from the merchant's account and return them to yours. Merchants hate chargebacks because they not only lose the money but are also hit with non-refundable dispute fees from the card networks (Visa/Mastercard).

When Can You File a Chargeback?

A chargeback should be your last resort, but it is highly effective. You can use this generator to dispute a charge if Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico):

  • Canceled Your Flight: They canceled your flight or significantly changed the schedule, and they refuse to refund you (offering a voucher instead). This falls under "Services Not Provided."
  • Promised a Refund but Didn't Pay: They agreed to refund you in an email or over the phone, but 15+ days have passed and the money hasn't appeared on your statement. This falls under "Credit Not Processed."
  • Severely Misrepresented a Hotel: The hotel you arrived at was uninhabitable (e.g., bedbugs, no AC, unsafe conditions) and you had to leave. This falls under "Services Not As Described."

The "Good Faith Effort" Rule

You cannot simply call your bank the second a flight is canceled. Federal law requires that you make a "Good Faith Effort" to resolve the dispute directly with Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico) first. You must send the merchant an email or call them demanding a refund. Only if they refuse or ignore you should you file the chargeback. When you submit the letter generated above to your bank, make sure to attach screenshots of your emails with the merchant to prove you tried!

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Will my bank definitely give me my money back?

Not automatically. The bank acts as a neutral judge. They will send your dispute to Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico), who has the right to reply and provide evidence. If your evidence is stronger (e.g., you have an email from the airline stating the flight was canceled), the bank will rule in your favor.

Does this work with Debit Cards?

Debit cards are governed by the Electronic Fund Transfer Act (EFTA), not the FCBA. While many banks offer dispute processes for debit cards that mimic credit card chargebacks, the legal protections are much weaker. This is why you should always book travel with a credit card.

What if I booked through an Online Travel Agency (OTA) like Expedia?

If you booked through a third party, look at your bank statement. Whoever's name is next to the charge is the "Merchant of Record." If it says "Expedia," you file the chargeback against Expedia, not the airline. If it says "Delta Airlines," you file it against Delta.

Important Info Regarding Aerovías de México, S.A. de C.V. (Aeroméxico) Disputes

Aeroméxico is headquartered in Mexico City and operates its main hub at Mexico City International Airport, connecting extensively with U.S. cities as part of its joint venture with Delta Air Lines. Mexican consumer protection law (PROFECO) provides separate remedies within Mexico, but U.S. cardholders disputing a charge should rely on standard FCBA grounds and document any refund refusal in writing.
