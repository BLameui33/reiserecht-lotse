Reporting Hotel Issues Booked Through Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.)

Booking a vacation through massive Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) like Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.) is incredibly popular. But what happens when you arrive and the hotel looks nothing like the catalog pictures? If your room is infested with mold, the construction noise prevents you from sleeping, or the AC is broken, you are entitled to compensation. In the past, OTAs would easily brush off complaints, but modern consumer protection practices, particularly regarding credit card disputes, have shifted the power back to the traveler.

Stopping the "Blame Game" with the Merchant of Record

When you complain to a booking portal, their standard response is often: "We are just the intermediary, please contact the hotel." The hotel, on the other hand, will tell you: "You booked through a third party, you have to get your refund from them." To end this frustrating ping-pong game, you must look at who actually charged your credit card. If Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.) is listed on your bank statement, they are the "Merchant of Record". They took your money, so they are ultimately responsible for delivering the services as described. By sending this formal demand, you force the OTA to contact the hotel management on your behalf.

How Much Money Are You Entitled To?

While some countries have strict legal tables for this, in the US, refund requests are based on industry standards and reasonable consumer expectations. Our calculator determines a realistic, percentage-based partial refund. For example, a missing ocean view might justify a 10% refund, while severe construction noise or a broken AC could warrant up to 20% off your daily rate. Requesting a calculated, realistic amount (instead of aggressively demanding a 100% refund for a minor issue) significantly increases your chances of the OTA processing the refund—or your bank siding with you during a chargeback dispute.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Who actually pays the refund?

Ultimately, the actual hotel property or management group absorbs the cost of the refund. However, because you booked via Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.), the portal must process the transaction back to your card. This letter instructs the portal to secure the funds from the hotel and pass them back to you.

Do I have to report the issue on-site?

Yes, this is extremely important! To succeed with a complaint or a subsequent credit card dispute, you must prove that you notified the local hotel management during your stay. You must have given the front desk an opportunity to remedy the situation (e.g., by moving you to a different room). If you say nothing and only complain after returning home, your claim will likely be denied.

What is a "Good Faith Effort"?

If Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.) ignores your request and you decide to initiate a chargeback with your bank, the bank will ask if you tried to resolve the issue with the merchant first. This generated PDF serves as your documented "Good Faith Effort," proving you formally requested a refund before escalating to a dispute.

Important Info Regarding Cheapflights (Booking Holdings Inc.) Refunds

Cheapflights, part of Booking Holdings Inc., operates as a travel search engine and booking intermediary rather than a direct supplier of travel services. The platform connects consumers with airlines, hotels, and other online agencies where the actual booking contract is formed. If you experience flight cancellations, hotel discrepancies, or other travel deficiencies, claims must be brought directly against the partner provider. Cheapflights does not manage reservations or process refunds. Corporate matters and formal disputes involving their US operations are handled through their affiliate network in Connecticut.

Legal Framework & Dispute Rights

The strategies and suggested procedures in this generator are based on US consumer protection laws and credit card network rules:

  • Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA): A US federal law that allows consumers to dispute credit card charges for goods and services that were not delivered as agreed or not provided as described.
    Official Information via the FTC
  • Card Network Dispute Rules (Visa / Mastercard): The internal rules governing chargebacks. The most relevant dispute condition for hotel complaints is "Services Not Provided or Merchandise/Services Not as Described" (e.g., Visa Reason Code 13.3).
