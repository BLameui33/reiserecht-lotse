Lost or Damaged Baggage: What You Are Entitled To and How to Proceed

For air travel, liability for destruction, loss, damage, and delay of checked baggage depends on the type of flight. For international flights, airlines are generally strictly liable under the Montreal Convention without needing to prove fault (Article 17). The liability is limited:, the maximum amount is 1,519 Special Drawing Rights (SDR) per passenger, roughly $2,000 to $2,200 depending on the exchange rate. This amount applies jointly to loss, damage, and delay. For US domestic flights, the US Department of Transportation (DOT) sets a higher maximum liability of $4,700 per passenger under 14 CFR Part 254.

First Steps at the Airport

Before leaving the baggage claim area, go to the airline or ground handler counter and have a damage or loss report filed (industry term: "Property Irregularity Report" or PIR). Note the reference number and keep your baggage tag and boarding pass. For damaged baggage, photograph the suitcase and contents at the baggage carousel; for lost baggage, try to secure a photo of your suitcase if one exists.

Depreciated Value Instead of Replacement Cost

Airlines typically do not reimburse the replacement cost but rather the depreciated value of the items at the time of loss. The older and more heavily used an item is, the lower its value. This tool calculates the depreciated value from the purchase price, age, and an annual depreciation rate. The preset percentages are common guidance values but not an official table. You can adjust each value.

Gathering Evidence When Receipts Are Missing

Airlines will ask for proof of value. If you don't have receipts, use bank statements, credit card statements, online order confirmations, warranty cards, photos of the items, or screenshots of product pages. Note the brand and model as precisely as possible. The more credible your evidence, the better your chances of acceptance.

Valuables and Electronics in Checked Baggage

Many airline conditions of carriage exclude valuables, cash, jewelry, or electronics in checked baggage from liability or recommend carrying them in your carry-on. Whether such clauses are enforceable in individual cases is disputed. For carry-on baggage, the airline is liable only in case of fault under the Montreal Convention. Travel baggage insurance or, depending on your contract, off-premises coverage under your homeowners or renters insurance can fill this gap. Check exclusions and maximum limits for valuables there.

Delayed Baggage: Emergency Purchases

If your baggage arrives days late, you may buy the most essential replacements, such as clothing and toiletries. Buy reasonably, keep all receipts, and report the delay immediately at the airport. In the tool, you can record emergency purchases via "Add emergency purchase with receipt". They are added to the list at the receipt amount.

Baggage Insurance or Airline: Which Route?

You can use both the airline and insurance, but you cannot be compensated twice for the same loss. Insurers often first require proof that you have filed a claim with the airline. Select the recipient above, and the letter will adapt accordingly. Later inform the insurance what the airline has reimbursed.

Package Tours vs. Individual Flights

If the flight is part of a package tour, missing baggage may also constitute a defect in the travel package. Contact your tour operator, as you may be entitled to a price reduction under applicable consumer protection law.

If the Airline Refuses or Does Not Respond

Request a written explanation. You can then file a complaint with the US Department of Transportation Aviation Consumer Protection Division, or for international flights, with the relevant aviation authority. You may also pursue small claims court or mediation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about Baggage Claims

How much compensation can I get from the airline for lost baggage?

For international flights, airlines are generally strictly liable under the Montreal Convention up to a maximum of 1,519 Special Drawing Rights per passenger - roughly $2,000 to $2,200 depending on the exchange rate. For US domestic flights, the US DOT sets a maximum liability of $4,700 per passenger under 14 CFR Part 254. Airlines typically reimburse the depreciated value, not the replacement cost.

Will I be reimbursed at replacement value or depreciated value?

Typically at the depreciated value, which reflects the item's worth at the time of loss considering age and wear. This tool calculates the depreciated value from the purchase price, age, and an annual depreciation rate that you can adjust.

What deadlines must I observe when filing a baggage claim?

For international flights under the Montreal Convention: damaged baggage must be reported in writing within 7 days of receipt; delayed baggage within 21 days of delivery. For US domestic flights, most carriers require written claims within 45 days for damaged/missing bags and 24 hours to 3 days for delayed baggage. Lawsuits must generally be filed within two years. Always report the issue at the airport immediately.

What if I no longer have purchase receipts?

Collect substitute evidence such as bank statements, online order confirmations, warranty cards, photos, or screenshots of brand and model information. The more credible and detailed your documentation, the more likely the airline will accept it. Reimbursement without proof is not guaranteed.

Can I be reimbursed for valuables and electronics in checked baggage?

Many airline conditions of carriage exclude valuables, cash, or electronics in checked baggage from liability, or recommend carrying them in your carry-on. Whether such exclusions are enforceable in individual cases is disputed. Baggage insurance can fill this gap if its terms cover valuables.

What applies to delayed baggage and emergency purchases?

You can claim reimbursement for reasonable replacement purchases due to delayed delivery if you keep the receipts. The maximum liability for baggage applies jointly to loss, damage, and delay. Purchase only essentials and report the delay immediately at the airport.

Important Notes Before Submitting

Send only copies of your evidence and keep the originals. Timestamp your message (email, online form with confirmation, or certified mail) and request acknowledgment of receipt. If the airline or insurer offers their own form, enter the items from your inventory list there and attach the PDF list in addition.
