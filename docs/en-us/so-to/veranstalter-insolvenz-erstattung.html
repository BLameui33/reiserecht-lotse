Travel company bankrupt: Your rights and the path to your money in the US

Unlike Europe, the United States does not have a federal insolvency protection scheme (like a mandatory guarantee fund) for package holidays. If a US travel supplier (airline, tour operator, hotel chain) files for Chapter 11 or Chapter 7 bankruptcy, consumers are generally considered "unsecured creditors". However, US consumers have powerful tools to recover their money, primarily through federal credit card laws and private travel insurance.

The FCBA: Your strongest weapon

The Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) is a federal law (15 U.S.C. § 1666) that protects consumers from being charged for "services not rendered". If a travel company goes bankrupt, the service is legally not rendered. You must send a written dispute letter to your card issuer's "Billing Inquiries" address within 60 days of the first statement containing the charge. During the investigation, the bank cannot report the amount as delinquent or charge interest on it. This is by far the fastest and most successful recovery method in the US.

Travel Insurance: Financial Default

Many comprehensive travel insurance policies include "Financial Default" coverage. This pays out if a supplier goes out of business. However, there is a catch: most policies only cover financial default if you purchased the insurance within 14 to 21 days of making your initial trip deposit. If you bought the insurance later, or if the supplier was already in financial distress when you booked, the claim may be denied.

The USTOA $1 Million Plan

If you booked with a member of the United States Tour Operators Association (USTOA), you are protected by their $1 Million Consumer Protection Plan. This fund is specifically designed to reimburse customers if a member company declares bankruptcy. You can file a claim directly through the USTOA, bypassing the bankruptcy court.

Bankruptcy Court: Proof of Claim

If you paid by debit card, bank transfer, or cash, and lack travel insurance, your only option is to file a Proof of Claim with the bankruptcy court handling the company's case. You will be classified as an "unsecured creditor". Unfortunately, unsecured creditors are at the bottom of the priority list. Recovery rates are typically very low (often pennies on the dollar), and the process can take years.

You are already travelling

If the bankruptcy happens mid-trip, contact your travel insurance provider's 24/7 assistance line immediately. If you paid by credit card, contact your issuer to dispute upcoming charges or request an emergency cash advance if your policy allows it. Keep every single receipt for out-of-pocket expenses (new hotels, last-minute flights) as you will need them for your FCBA dispute or insurance claim.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about US travel bankruptcies

Do I get my money back if a US travel company goes bankrupt?

Unlike Europe, the US has no federal guarantee fund for package holidays. If you paid by credit card, you are protected by the Fair Credit Billing Act (FCBA) and can dispute the charge as 'Services Not Rendered'. If you have travel insurance with 'Financial Default' coverage, you can file a claim. Otherwise, you become an unsecured creditor in bankruptcy court.

What is the FCBA and how does it help me?

The Fair Credit Billing Act (15 U.S.C. § 1666) allows you to dispute credit card charges for services not provided. If a travel supplier goes bankrupt, you can withhold payment. You must notify your card issuer in writing within 60 days of the first statement containing the charge.

Does travel insurance cover bankruptcy?

Many comprehensive travel insurance policies include 'Financial Default' coverage. However, it often requires that you purchased the policy within 14-21 days of making your initial trip deposit. Check your policy's specific terms regarding the supplier's bankruptcy.

What is a Proof of Claim in bankruptcy court?

If you paid by debit card, bank transfer, or lack insurance, you must file a 'Proof of Claim' (Form 410) with the bankruptcy court handling the company's case. You will be listed as an unsecured creditor. Recovery rates are typically very low and the process takes months or years.

What is the USTOA $1 Million Plan?

Members of the United States Tour Operators Association (USTOA) are required to post $1 million in security to cover customer refunds in the event of bankruptcy. If your tour operator is a USTOA member, you can file a claim directly through their Consumer Protection Plan.

What to do if I am already traveling when the bankruptcy happens?

Contact your travel insurance provider's 24/7 assistance line immediately. If you paid by credit card, contact your issuer to dispute upcoming charges. Keep all receipts for out-of-pocket expenses (hotels, flights) as you will need them for your FCBA dispute or insurance claim.

Important notices before sending

For FCBA disputes, you must send the letter to the specific "Billing Inquiries" address of your card issuer, not the general payment address. It is highly recommended to send the letter via USPS Certified Mail with Return Receipt to prove delivery within the 60-day window. Never send original documents, only copies.
