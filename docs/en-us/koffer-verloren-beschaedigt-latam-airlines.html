Lost or Damaged Luggage: Your Rights Under DOT Rules & the Montreal Convention

Depending on your travel route, your baggage rights are protected by two powerful legal frameworks. For domestic flights within the United States, the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) strictly mandates under Title 14 CFR Part 254 that airlines like LATAM Airlines Group S.A. are liable for verifiable baggage loss, delay, or damage up to $3,800 per passenger. For international flights, the Montreal Convention applies, capping liability at roughly 1,288 Special Drawing Rights (SDR), which equates to about $1,700 per passenger. Regardless of the route, airlines are strictly liable for the mishandling of checked baggage.

Delayed Baggage: What qualifies as an "incidental expense"?

If you arrive at your vacation destination and your suitcase is missing, you are permitted to purchase essential items at the expense of LATAM Airlines Group S.A. (referred to as incidental expenses). The main legal principle here is the duty to mitigate damages. This means you cannot go on a luxury shopping spree. Reimbursements cover items absolutely necessary for the purpose of your trip. This includes toiletries (toothbrush, shampoo), underwear, and appropriate clothing (e.g., a replacement suit for an urgent business meeting or a swimsuit for a beach vacation). Keep all receipts safe and claim the expenses back using our template.

Total Loss: When the suitcase is gone forever

If your luggage does not turn up even after an intensive search, it is legally considered lost (total loss) after exactly 21 days. From day 21, you have the right to claim the full value (accounting for depreciation) of the suitcase and the entire contents from LATAM Airlines Group S.A., up to the maximum liability limit. It is best to create a detailed packing list from memory and attach any old purchase receipts if available.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about Baggage Claims

What happens if my luggage is delayed on the flight back home?

If you arrive home (at your primary residence) and the suitcase is missing, you are generally not allowed to make emergency purchases at the airline's expense. Airlines and the DOT assume you have sufficient clothes and toiletries in your closet at home. However, any permanent damage or the total loss of the suitcase will still be fully compensated.

How do I prove the value of my suitcase and belongings?

Ideally, you still have the purchase receipts for the suitcase or the valuable items inside. However, LATAM Airlines Group S.A. will usually apply a deduction for depreciation (wear and tear over the years of use). If the suitcase is physically destroyed, it is best to get a local luggage shop to confirm in writing that a repair is not economically viable.

Do I have to mail the original receipts to the airline?

No, never send original purchase receipts to the airline by mail! Always attach legible copies or scans to your claim letter. The airline is required to accept copies. Keep the originals safe at home in case you need to file a formal complaint with the DOT or pursue legal action.

Important Info Regarding LATAM Airlines Group S.A. Claims

LATAM Airlines Group is the largest airline group in Latin America, headquartered in Santiago, Chile, and a member of the Oneworld alliance, operating extensive routes between the U.S. and South America. On flights to or from the U.S., passengers are protected by DOT rules on refunds and denied boarding compensation, with the Montreal Convention applying to international baggage claims. Official Legal Framework

The claims and deadlines in this generator are based on the following federal regulations and international agreements regarding baggage liability:

  • U.S. Department of Transportation (Title 14 CFR Part 254): The federal regulation that establishes the maximum liability limit for lost, damaged, or delayed baggage on domestic flights (currently up to $3,800).

  • Montreal Convention (MC99) (esp. Art. 17, 19 and 22): The binding international treaty governing airline liability for baggage issues on international itineraries.
