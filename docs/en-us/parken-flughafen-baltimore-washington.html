Why Parking Directly at the Terminal is a Price Trap

Airport operators shamelessly exploit the convenience of passengers. The parking garages right next to the departure halls are primarily aimed at business travelers whose companies cover the costs, or at people doing short pick-ups and drop-offs. If you park there as a family for a two-week summer vacation, you often pay more than for an extra hotel room. Even the so-called "economy lots" on distant official airport property are usually more expensive than independent, private parking operators in the surrounding area.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) about Parking at Baltimore/Washington International

How much time should I plan for the shuttle service?

We recommend adding about 30 to 45 minutes on top of your airline's regular check-in time. The drive from the lot to the BWI terminal usually only takes 5 to 12 minutes, but loading/unloading luggage takes a moment, and the shuttle might currently be on another run.

What happens if my return flight is delayed?

That is not a problem. When booking, you provide your return flight number. The alternative parking providers track flights live. The shuttle bus knows exactly when you will land, even if you are delayed by several hours. If your return is pushed to the next day, a small fee for the extra day will apply (usually around $5–$10), which you simply pay on-site.

Do I have to hand over my car keys?

That depends on the chosen parking lot. With valet parking, handing over the keys is mandatory since the employee parks the car for you. With classic shuttle parking, most portals offer filter options like "keep your keys". In that case, you park the car yourself and take the keys with you on your trip.
