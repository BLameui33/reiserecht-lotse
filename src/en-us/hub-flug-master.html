Flight Delay & Cancellation: How to Claim up to $1,550 in Compensation & Refunds

A delayed or canceled flight can completely ruin your travel plans. However, as a passenger, you have specific rights under US Department of Transportation (DOT) regulations. Many travelers do not realize that they are entitled to a mandatory full refund for significant delays or cancellations if they choose not to fly, and up to $1,550 in cash compensation if they are involuntarily bumped due to overbooking.

Airlines often try to avoid their obligations by citing "uncontrollable events" like weather. In reality, however, technical problems and crew shortages are legally considered controllable issues, meaning the airline owes you care and assistance. Checklist: Immediate Help for Flight Problems

If you are currently stuck at the airport, follow these steps to secure your future claim for compensation or a refund:

Checklist

  • Get the reason confirmed: Ask the airline staff for a written statement detailing the exact reason for the delay.
  • Collect receipts: Keep your boarding passes, booking confirmations, and receipts for out-of-pocket food or hotel expenses.
  • Take photos: Document the departure board showing your delayed or canceled flight.
  • Demand care and assistance: For controllable delays, request meal vouchers and hotel accommodations directly from the gate agent.
  • Do not sign travel vouchers blindly: Do not sign or accept flight credits if doing so means waiving your right to a full cash refund.

Case Studies: When the Airline Must Pay

Example 1: Maintenance Issue on a Domestic Flight

A flight from New York to Chicago is delayed by 4 hours due to a defective hydraulic system. The airline claims this was a sudden issue.
Result: Under US DOT rules, maintenance is a "controllable" delay. The passenger is entitled to meal vouchers and, if they choose to cancel their trip, an automatic full refund to their original payment method.

Example 2: Involuntary Bumping (Overbooking)

A traveler is flying from Los Angeles to Miami. The flight is overbooked, and the passenger is denied boarding against their will. The airline rebooks them, but they arrive 3 hours later than originally planned.
Result: Since the arrival delay is over 2 hours for a domestic flight, the DOT mandates maximum compensation. Claim: 400% of the one-way fare, up to $1,550.

FAQ: Your Rights in Detail

When are circumstances truly "uncontrollable"?
Only events that the airline cannot reasonably prevent, such as severe weather systems, natural disasters, or Air Traffic Control (ATC) disruptions. Maintenance issues, aircraft changes, or a shortage of airline crew are considered controllable by the airline and trigger passenger assistance commitments.
How far back can I retroactively claim a refund or compensation?
Unlike specific federal aviation limits, retroactive claims often fall under breach of contract law. In the US, the statute of limitations varies by state but generally ranges from 2 to 6 years. However, it is always best to file complaints with the airline or the DOT as soon as possible.
What happens if I am offered an alternative flight?
If your flight is significantly delayed or canceled and the airline offers alternative transportation, you can decline it and demand a full refund instead. For overbooking situations, if the alternative flight gets you to your destination within 1 hour of the original time, no bumping compensation is owed.
