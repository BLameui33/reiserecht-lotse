Steps Against the OTA Delay Tactic

  • Get airline confirmation: Get written confirmation (email/chat) from the airline that the refund has already been issued to the travel agent.
  • No vouchers: Do not accept "Wallet Credits" or portal vouchers. US DOT rules entitle you to cash back to your original payment method.
  • Set a deadline: Use our tool to generate a 14-day deadline notice and email it to the portal.
  • Prepare a chargeback: Document all your attempts to contact them. If the deadline passes, contact your bank/credit card issuer for a chargeback.
  • Legal consequences: Inform the portal that if the deadline passes, they will be held liable for breach of contract and potential small claims court fees.

FAQ: Help with the OTA Trap

Why shouldn't I just sue the airline directly?
If the airline has already returned the money to the intermediary (OTA), it has fulfilled its legal duty. Your claim is then against the portal, which is holding your money as the "merchant of record" (unjust enrichment).
How do I find the correct address for the portal?
Many portals are headquartered overseas or have complex corporate structures. The official addresses of the parent companies are already pre-filled for you in our generators.
What can consumer protection agencies do?
Filing a complaint with the US Department of Transportation (DOT) Aviation Consumer Protection Division or the Better Business Bureau (BBB) is free and often forces OTAs to respond. However, sending a legally sound demand letter yourself is always the first and most important step to establish a formal default.
