Airline Ignoring You? How to Escalate to the DOT

TL;DR (Quick Answer): If an airline unfairly denies a legally required refund or compensation, you can force a response by filing a complaint with the US Department of Transportation (DOT). Federal law requires the airline to provide a substantive response within 60 days. Use our tool below to draft a professional escalation letter.

If you have requested a refund for a canceled flight or compensation for being bumped and the airline simply ignores you or wrongly denies your claim, you do not have to accept it. The US Department of Transportation (DOT) has strict consumer protection rules that airlines must follow.

Filing a formal complaint with the DOT puts massive pressure on the airline. By law, the airline must acknowledge your complaint within 30 days and provide a substantive response within 60 days. Our generator helps you create a structured escalation letter to submit to the airline and the DOT.

Checklist: Before You Involve the DOT

To ensure your escalation is successful, make sure you have completed the following steps:

DOT Escalation Checklist

  • Contact the airline first: The DOT requires that you try to resolve the issue with the airline directly before filing a complaint.
  • Keep your initial request: Save the email or support ticket number of your first refund request.
  • Gather proof: Have your ticket number, booking confirmation, and any communication with the airline ready.
  • Submit via the official DOT portal: After generating your text with our tool, copy it into the official DOT online complaint form.

Case Studies: Successful DOT Escalations

Example: The Ignored Refund Request

A passenger's domestic flight is canceled by the airline due to a crew shortage. The passenger requests a cash refund online, but the airline ignores the emails for three months, only offering automated replies.
Solution: The passenger files a DOT complaint. Because airlines face heavy fines for violating the DOT’s refund mandate, the airline's executive resolution team steps in and issues the full cash refund to the original credit card within 14 days.

Example: The Illegal Voucher for Bumping

A traveler is involuntarily denied boarding (bumped) from an oversold flight. The gate agent hands them a $500 travel voucher. However, federal law states they are entitled to a cash check of up to $1,550.
Solution: The traveler escalates the violation to the DOT using our template. The DOT forces the airline to explain the violation. The airline quickly invalidates the voucher and sends the legally required cash compensation.

FAQ: The Escalation Process

What happens after I file a DOT complaint?
The DOT forwards your complaint directly to the airline. The airline is federally mandated to review your case and respond. Because a high volume of DOT complaints can lead to heavy fines for the airline, your case is usually handled by higher-level support staff rather than automated bots.
Does it cost money to file a DOT complaint?
No, filing a complaint with the US Department of Transportation is a completely free service for consumers. Our document generator is also completely free to use.

DOT Passenger Rights Glossary

  • Substantive Response: A legally required reply from an airline to a DOT complaint that directly addresses the specific issues raised, rather than a generic automated message.
  • Involuntary Denied Boarding (IDB): The technical term for when a passenger is "bumped" from a flight against their will because the airline oversold the plane.
  • Significant Schedule Change: The threshold (now defined by DOT rules) at which a schedule change or delay entitles a passenger to a full refund if they choose not to travel.
