Car hire: how to fight back against rip-offs and unfair charges

A cheap online deal often turns into an expensive trap at your holiday destination. Many car hire companies lure customers in with extremely low daily rates, only to make money at the desk through aggressive sales of unnecessary add-on insurance, upgrades or unclear fuel policies.

There are also increasing numbers of cases where holidaymakers are later charged for alleged scratches or stone chips. On this page, we explain which insurance you really need (for example, a high third-party liability limit and fully comprehensive cover with no excess) and how to challenge unfair charges made to your credit card successfully.

Checklist: safe collection and return

Do not let yourself be pressured at the desk, and document the vehicle’s condition meticulously:

Checklist

  • Record existing damage: Check the car for scratches, dents and stone chips when collecting it. Make sure EVERY single mark, however small, is entered on the collection report.
  • Take photos & videos: Film the car from all sides when collecting and returning it (including the roof, underside and wheels) in good lighting conditions.
  • Photograph the fuel level: Take a photo of the fuel gauge on collection and again immediately on return in the car park.
  • No unnecessary signatures: Do not sign contracts in languages you do not understand. Check that no extras (for example, "Super CDW") have been ticked if you do not want them.
  • Ask for a return report: When returning the car, insist that a member of staff checks it and gives you a signed "Damage Free" report.

Case studies: the tricks used by hire companies

Example 1: the insurance bluff at the desk

Mr Weber booked a car in Mallorca through a portal, including "fully comprehensive cover with no excess". At the desk, the staff member says: "That insurance does not apply with us; in the event of damage we will block €1,500. You must take out our in-house insurance for €25/day."
The truth: Mr Weber is in fact covered. In the event of damage, he may have to pay the amount upfront locally, but he will be reimbursed afterwards by the online portal (the broker). He confidently declines the add-on insurance.

Example 2: alleged scratch after return

Ms Schneider returns her hire car in Italy early in the morning and drops the key into the drop box because the desk is still closed. A week later, £390 is charged to her credit card for an alleged scratch on the bumper.
The solution: Ms Schneider filmed the undamaged vehicle at the airport when returning it. She uses our template letter, sends the video to the provider as evidence and at the same time requests a chargeback from her bank. The money is refunded.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about car hire

Do I have to take out the expensive add-on insurance at the desk?
No. If you booked your hire car through a portal (broker) including "fully comprehensive cover with no excess", you are already covered. Staff on site often try to sell their own heavily overpriced policies by using scare tactics. Decline them politely but firmly.
What should I do if I am charged for damage after returning the car that I did not cause?
Dispute the charge immediately in writing. Ask the car hire company to provide the signed return report showing the vehicle was returned without defects, as well as the repair assessment. Submit your own photos as evidence, taken when you returned the car. In urgent cases, you may be able to reverse an unauthorised card charge through your credit card provider (chargeback).
Why is my card often not accepted for the deposit?
Many providers require a genuine credit card with an available credit limit in order to block the deposit. Debit cards (for example modern Visa/Mastercard Debit or Maestro), where the money is taken directly from your current account, are often refused at the desk. The trick: the provider then pressures you into taking out expensive insurance for which no deposit is required.
Which fuel policy is best?
Always choose the "Full to Full" policy. You collect the car with a full tank and return it full. With "Full to Empty", you pay extremely inflated per-litre prices for the first tank plus a hefty service charge, and you also lose any fuel left in the tank at the end.
