Travel Credit Cards: How to Avoid the Foreign Currency Trap

If you spend your holiday outside the euro area or the UK (for example in Turkey, Asia or the USA), paying and withdrawing cash can easily become an expensive trap. Traditional banks almost always charge a non-sterling transaction fee (around 1.75% - 2.00%) for currency conversion, as well as hefty flat fees at cash machines.

With a dedicated travel credit card, these worries can become a thing of the past. In our guide, you will learn how to avoid the nasty "DCC conversion trap" at cash machines, what charges your current bank may be imposing, and which free credit cards let you access cash worldwide without fees.

The Ultimate Travel Finance Checklist

To protect your travel budget and make sure your cards work properly abroad, go through these points before you fly:

Checklist: Finances & Cards Abroad

  • Apply for a travel credit card in good time: Apply for a permanently fee-free credit card (for example Barclays, Chase UK or another travel card provider) at least 3 weeks before departure so that the card and PIN arrive in time by post.
  • Check card restrictions with your bank: Some banks block cards for use outside Europe or in certain regions. Give them a quick call and ask for your destination country to be enabled if necessary.
  • Know the DCC trap at cash machines: If the cash machine asks whether you want to be charged in "pounds sterling" or the "local currency": ALWAYS choose the local currency! (Otherwise you could lose up to 10% through poor exchange rates).
  • Remember your PIN: Abroad (especially in the USA or the UK), you are often asked for your PIN when paying by credit card, even if you usually pay contactlessly or sign elsewhere.
  • Take two cards with you: Always carry at least two cards from different schemes (for example 1x Visa, 1x Mastercard) and keep them separate from each other for theft protection.

Case Studies: How Much Non-Sterling Transaction Fees Can Cost

Case Study 1: Family Holiday in Turkey

The Smith family spend 14 days in Antalya. They pay for excursions, restaurants and souvenirs (around £1,750 in total) using their high street bank card in Turkish lira. In addition, the father withdraws cash 4 times from a cash machine.
The cost trap: The bank charges a 1.75% non-sterling transaction fee (£30.63) and £5 per withdrawal (£20). In total, the family wastes £50.63 on unnecessary bank charges that could have been avoided with a free travel credit card.

Case Study 2: Road Trip Across the USA

Sarah takes a 3-week road trip through California. In the USA, even the smallest coffee is often paid for by card (around £3,000 total spend). She also hires a car, for which a deposit of $1,000 must be pre-authorised.
The solution: Sarah arranged a "proper" travel credit card in advance (credit, not debit). This saves her more than £55 in foreign currency charges. In addition, the car hire deposit is pre-authorised without any issues (with debit cards, the deposit is often refused or handled less smoothly).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) About Travel Credit Cards

What is a non-sterling transaction fee?
A non-sterling transaction fee (also called a foreign currency fee) is a charge, usually around 1.75% to 2.00%, that traditional banks apply when you pay or withdraw cash in a currency other than pounds sterling.
Should I choose pounds sterling or the local currency at a cash machine abroad?
Always choose to be charged in the local currency (for example baht, lira or dollars) and firmly decline conversion into pounds sterling ("Conversion to GBP")! So-called dynamic currency conversion (DCC) used by cash machine operators often applies very poor exchange rates and can cost you up to 10% in hidden charges.
Why is my normal debit card not enough?
Outside Europe, some traditional debit card schemes may not be accepted at all. Even if they do work, your bank may add a flat fee of £4 to £6 per withdrawal plus percentage-based foreign currency charges. A good travel credit card removes these charges entirely.
Are permanently fee-free travel credit cards really free?
Yes. Cards from providers such as Barclays, Chase UK or other travel card issuers may have no annual fee. Providers make money through interchange fees (paid by the retailer when you shop) or through credit interest if you set your repayment option to instalments instead of 100% full repayment.
