Roadmap for Travel Law Complaints: How to Enforce Your Claims

Whether it is a flight delay, flight cancellation, lost luggage, hotel defect, or package holiday cancellation: consumers often face a wall of silence or template responses when dealing with large corporations and booking platforms. On this directory portal, you will find specific guides for dozens of airlines, tour operators, and booking agents.

The 4 Stages of Successful Complaint Escalation

  1. Direct request with a specific deadline: Submit your claim in writing (quoting your booking reference, flight number, or reservation number) and set a reasonable deadline (typically 14 to 21 days).
  2. Evidence gathering & completeness: Attach receipts, boarding passes, proof of expenses for replacement services (e.g. food, hotel, taxi), and photos directly to your initial communication.
  3. Properly evaluate a rejection: If an airline cites "extraordinary circumstances" (such as severe weather or strikes) or an agent redirects you to the end provider, check our specific guides to see if this excuse holds up legally.
  4. Escalate to an ombudsman or legal action: If the provider fails to respond or rejects your claim unjustly, you can escalate free of charge to an officially recognized dispute resolution body (ADR/ombudsman) or instruct a passenger rights portal / solicitor.

What rights are you entitled to depending on your travel type?

  • Air travel (UK261 / EU Regulation EC 261/2004): Right to £220 (€250) up to £520 (€600) compensation for delays of 3 hours or more, cancellations, or overbooking, as well as care and assistance on-site.
  • Package holidays (Cancellation & Price Reduction): Right to a price reduction in the event of defects on-site (using standard legal frameworks / holiday defect tables) or fee-free cancellation in the event of unavoidable, extraordinary circumstances at the destination.
  • Baggage issues (Montreal Convention): Up to approx. £1,400 (€1,600) reimbursement for damaged, delayed, or lost luggage, provided the damage is reported within the required deadlines.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ) on Submitting Complaints

How long do I have to submit a complaint or claim?

In the UK, statutory limitation periods for travel contract claims and passenger rights generally allow up to 6 years (3 years under German civil law, ending at the end of the year). However, extremely short reporting deadlines apply to baggage damage: delayed baggage must be reported in writing to the airline within 21 days, and damaged baggage within 7 days.

What should I do if the airline or hotel does not respond at all?

If there is no response after the deadline you set (usually 2 to 3 weeks) has expired, the provider is in default. You can then open a free dispute resolution procedure with the relevant ADR scheme or ombudsman service (such as AviationADR, CEDR, or public transport conciliation bodies) or hand the matter over to a solicitor or specialized claims portal.

Do conciliation bodies or ombudsman services cost money for consumers?

No, conciliation and dispute resolution procedures with approved consumer ombudsman bodies are completely free of charge for passengers and travellers. The costs are borne by the participating companies as required by law.

Why does the complaint process differ depending on the provider?

Many airlines and travel portals use their own online forms and refuse processing via simple email. In addition, responsible dispute resolution bodies, customer service addresses, and registered head offices vary (especially for foreign airlines).
