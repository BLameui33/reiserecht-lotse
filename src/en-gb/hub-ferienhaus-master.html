Holiday Let Defects: Your Rights to a Rent Reduction

The photos showed a dream villa, but the reality is very different: a green pool, mould on the walls, pests or a faulty heating system during a winter break. If your booked holiday house or holiday flat has significant defects, your rights will usually arise under your accommodation contract, the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 and, where applicable, the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018.

The law is on your side here: if the accommodation does not match the promised description, you may be entitled to a price reduction. The biggest mistake: travellers put up with the problem for two weeks, go home and only then ask the portal for money back. That usually does not work. You must give the owner or provider the chance to put things right straight away while you are there. Use our free generators to pursue industry-standard reduction rates.

Checklist: What to do on site if your holiday let has defects

Anyone who accepts defects without complaint may lose their right to a refund. Be sure to follow this checklist:

Checklist for holiday lets

  • Report the defect immediately: Contact the owner, property manager or local key-handover point straight away.
  • Inform the portal: At the same time, send a message through the provider’s messaging system (e.g. Novasol, Booking) to document that you reported it in time.
  • Request a remedy & set a deadline: Ask for an immediate repair (e.g. pool cleaning, heating repair) or cleaning.
  • Preserve evidence: Take clear photos and videos (with date stamp) of dirt, mould or defects.
  • Note witnesses: Write down the names and addresses of witnesses (e.g. neighbours or fellow travellers who are not family members).

Case studies: Entitlement to a reduction under consumer law

Example 1: Pool unusable

The Becker family books a finca in Mallorca for €2,500. The pool, advertised as a key feature, is completely dirty on arrival and the pump is broken. Despite immediate reporting to the agency, nothing happens for days.
Outcome: The loss of a promised key facility such as a swimming pool may justify a 15% to 20% reduction for the period of the defect, depending on the circumstances and with reference to industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation.

Example 2: Heating failure during a winter break

A married couple rents a cabin in Denmark in December. The heating fails completely on the second day and the indoor temperature drops to 12 degrees. The caretaker cannot be reached.
Outcome: A total heating failure in winter is a serious defect. If the property is practically uninhabitable, the provider may face up to a 100% reduction for the affected days. The guests may even move to a hotel and claim the additional costs.

Frequently asked questions (FAQ) about holiday let defects

Do package travel rules apply to holiday lets?
No, usually not. If you are only renting accommodation (without additional travel services such as flights as part of a package), the matter will generally be governed by your accommodation contract and the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015. Liability for defects depends on whether the property matches the condition contractually promised.
Who do I need to report the defect in the holiday let to?
You must report the defect promptly on site. Your first point of contact is the direct owner, the local property manager or the key agency named in the contract. For evidential reasons, you should also report it immediately to the booking portal in writing.
Can I use the Frankfurter Tabelle for holiday flats?
No. The Frankfurter Tabelle is a German framework for package travel and does not apply in the UK. In practice, industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation may be used as a helpful reference point when estimating an appropriate reduction. Our generators are guided by these types of market-standard benchmarks.
Who should I address my claim letter to?
This can often be legally complex. Portals (such as FeWo-direkt or Casamundo) usually act only as intermediaries, while the legal contracting party is the property owner. For practical reasons, however, we recommend first pursuing the reduction through the portal or intermediary, as they often have internal complaints procedures or hold funds (such as deposits). The PDF tool therefore addresses the selected portal.
