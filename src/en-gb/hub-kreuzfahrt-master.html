Cruise defects: How to claim a holiday price reduction (ABTA compensation guidelines)

A cruise is usually an expensive dream holiday. If the cabin does not meet the expected standard, the itinerary is changed at short notice, or there is unbearable construction noise on deck, the frustration is considerable. What many guests do not realise is that a cruise is generally treated in law as a package holiday. That means you are well protected by the UK Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018.

Instead of letting the guest relations desk fob you off with a free cocktail or £50 onboard credit, you may have the right to a proper cash refund. The industry-standard ABTA guidelines for compensation serve as a practical legal benchmark and often support refunds of 10% up to 30% of the proportionate holiday price where there are significant defects. Checklist: Immediate help for cruise defects

The most important thing with travel defects is preserving evidence on the spot. Follow these steps on board to support your later price reduction claim:

Checklist: Defects on board

  • Report the defect: Report the problem IMMEDIATELY to reception or guest relations. If you keep quiet about the defect, you may weaken your later claim.
  • Set a deadline for remedy: Ask for a solution (for example, a different cabin) and note the name of the staff member.
  • Preserve evidence: Take photographs (for example of dirt or defects) or keep a detailed noise and odour log with dates and times.
  • Find witnesses: Exchange mobile numbers with other affected passengers. Statements from independent fellow travellers can be extremely valuable.
  • Do not sign final settlements: If you accept onboard credit, do not sign anything stating it is in full and final settlement of all claims; instead, note that you accept it without prejudice.

Case examples under ABTA compensation guidelines

Example 1: Itinerary change & missed port

On a Mediterranean cruise (total price £3,000 for 10 days = £300/day), the highlight stop at Santorini is cancelled due to strong winds without an equivalent replacement port.
Outcome: As the holiday contract was not delivered as agreed, the ABTA compensation guidelines may support a price reduction. For the lost day, the passenger will often be entitled to around 20 to 30% of the daily price (around £60 - £90 per person).

Example 2: Unacceptable noise in the cabin

The allocated cabin is directly below the ship’s nightclub. Sleeping is impossible until 3 am. Reception has no replacement cabin available.
Outcome: Noise that seriously disrupts night-time rest is a major defect. For the affected period, reductions of up to 30% of the proportionate holiday price may be claimed.

FAQ: Your rights in detail

What amount is the percentage applied to?
The reduction is always calculated proportionately. If the defect affects the whole holiday (for example, a missing balcony that was booked), the reduction is based on the total holiday price. If the problem only occurs on certain days (for example, 2 days of construction noise by the pool), the holiday price is divided by the number of travel days and the percentage is applied to that daily holiday price.
What happens if the cruise line claims force majeure?
For a holiday price reduction, arguments about force majeure (such as storms or dense fog) will not necessarily prevent a claim where the holiday was not provided as booked. A reduction may still be due if the contracted service was not delivered, even where the cruise line was not at fault. The position may be different for additional damages for loss of enjoyment.
Can I demand an outside cabin if my inside cabin is defective?
Yes. If the booked cabin category cannot be used because of a defect and no identical cabin is available, the cruise line should offer a free upgrade where reasonable. If the cruise line refuses and you have to remain in the defective cabin, your claim for a price reduction may increase significantly.
